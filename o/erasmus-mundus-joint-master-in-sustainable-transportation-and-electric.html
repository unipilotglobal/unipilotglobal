Erasmus Mundus Joint Master in Sustainable Transportation and Electric Power Systems (STEPS)

STEPS consortium (University of Oviedo, University of Kiel, Politécnico de Coimbra, Sapienza University of Rome)

What it coversErasmus Mundus scholarship of EUR 1,400 per month for up to 24 months plus a tuition fee waiver (full scholarships cover tuition fees plus a contribution to subsistence costs); the tuition fee for self-funded students is on the programme's tuition page
DeadlineOct – Jan (annual; the programme's standing timetable publishes applications on 15 Oct and closes them on 15 Jan for the September start, with results in March and May)
Host countrySpain, Germany, Portugal, Italy
SubjectsEngineering & Technology, Environment, Water & Energy
Application effortVery High: Online application + degree certificates and transcripts + English test + CV + motivation letter (+ references)
Best suited toEngineering graduates who want a fully funded joint master's in electric transport and power systems across Spain, Germany, Portugal and Italy

Details checked on 30 October 2026. Dates and amounts change every year. Always confirm on the official page.

Who can apply

A bachelor's degree in Engineering with at least three years of university study (at least 180 ECTS) and English at about B2 level (for example IELTS Academic 6 with no part below 5.5, TOEFL iBT 79, or PTE Academic 55). Scholarships are open to Partner Country and Programme Country nationals under the Erasmus Mundus rules: full-time study only, no other EU grant for the same course, and at most two scholarship students of the same nationality. You indicate on the form whether you are applying for a scholarship.

About this opportunity

A 2-year joint master's in sustainable transportation and electric power systems taught by the Universities of Oviedo, Kiel, Coimbra (Politécnico) and Sapienza Rome. Students choose whether to apply for an Erasmus Mundus scholarship on the single application form.

Tips from our advisors

  1. Read the eligibility line by line before you write anything. Most rejections are eligibility mismatches, not weak essays.
  2. Show a clear link between your past studies, this programme and your career goal. Committees look for a story, not a list.
  3. Start at least 8 to 12 weeks early. Certified transcripts, translations and referee letters usually take longer than expected.
  4. Ask your referees at least 4 weeks ahead and give them your CV, your draft SOP and the deadline.
  5. Submit at least 48 hours before the deadline. Portals slow down on the last day.
  6. Have your SOP and CV reviewed by someone who has seen successful applications before you submit.
